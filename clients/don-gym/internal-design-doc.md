## Problem Statement

Sameer has a warm consulting opportunity with Don, owner of a 3-location NJ gym. Don has said the words "let me know how much you charge" but has not asked for a specific proposal or scope. Sameer has been embedded in weekly Wellness Living account-manager calls as a gym member, has unusually deep visibility into the operation, and is mid-migration support.

The challenge: convert this informal opening into a paid, scoped, repeatable consulting engagement that becomes the template for Sameer's AI Agency. Don is reluctant to share membership data, which signals an emotional trust gap inconsistent with his stated buying intent.

The deliverable Sameer asked for: a one-page growth strategy that excites Don about what's possible AND closes the engagement. Not a tactical proposal, not a pure vision deck — a staged growth roadmap with the wedge as the entry point.

---

## Demand Evidence

- Don said: **"let me know how much you charge"** — direct buying-intent statement, not as strong as "send me a proposal" but stronger than "I'd love your thoughts"
- Don has named the pain himself: "we can't answer simple things like how many members do we have by location"
- Don has a budget for related work: $10K marketing campaign, 3 months, focused on Eatontown and Freehold
- Don has been bringing Sameer into vendor calls — implicit endorsement of Sameer's strategic value
- **Counter-signal:** Don is reluctant to share membership data. This is an emotional trust gap inconsistent with stated buying intent. The proposal must work AROUND this gap, not pretend it doesn't exist.

## Status Quo

What Don has TODAY:

- Wellness Living platform across 3 locations: $400/mo each ($1,200/mo total)
- Wellness Living account manager embedded in weekly calls
- Migration from Mind Body started Oct 1, operational Feb 13, ~80% complete
- Old packages ($109 / $179) being converted to new ($99 / $149)
- Claudia handles member follow-up via Google Voice (deliverability + compliance issues)
- Constant Contact for email (significant spam-folder placement)
- HyRox + PT bookings handled manually by Don (not in Wellness Living)
- 3 separate Verizon FiOS phone lines, no central phone system
- Brivo (key fob access) at Freehold, separate from Wellness Living
- Mind Body legacy data quality issues (e.g., open gym scans logged as classes)
- ~500 members estimated, but member vs. membership distinction (family memberships) makes the count unreliable

The Wellness Living account manager owns INSIDE-platform work. The gap she structurally cannot fill: cross-system reconciliation, custom outbound automation, paid acquisition with attribution, integration of HyRox/PT into Wellness Living. THAT is the wedge.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** Don, gym owner. Specific job-to-be-done: stop the migration mess from limiting growth so the gym can grow members through a $10K paid acquisition campaign without leaking 70% of the inquiries.

**Narrowest wedge:** Lead-to-first-class loop — website inquiry → Claudia's outbound text on a compliant business number → booked free class → Wellness Living record with source attribution. Highest frequency, most measurable, structurally outside the WL account manager's jurisdiction. Don has already named this as broken ("most people don't respond," "lot of it was going into spam," "text response higher than email").

## Constraints

- Don is reluctant to share data without a paid, NDA-protected context
- Sameer doesn't yet have Wellness Living API access (asking the vendor)
- Sameer is solo, runs three other businesses concurrently — engagement must be scoped to fit
- Compliance overhead on SMS (10DLC registration for the campaign) takes weeks to clear
- The $10K campaign Don wants to run is contingent on the foundation work completing first; if Sameer can't deliver Phase 1 before the campaign launches, the campaign converts at 1/3 its potential
- Don has multiple parallel relationships (WL account manager, possibly other consultants Don hasn't disclosed) — Sameer must position as complementary, not competitive

## Premises

1. **Don is intellectually open to hiring Sameer but emotionally guarded about data exposure.** Validated by the contradiction between "let me know what you charge" and reluctance to share membership data. The proposal must structurally solve the trust gap, not just argue past it.
2. **The defensible wedge is cross-system, not inside-Wellness-Living.** Anything inside WL is the account manager's job and creates direct conflict. Anything between WL and other systems is structurally outside her jurisdiction.
3. **Phase 0 (paid discovery, ~$2.5K, NDA-protected) is the only entry that works.** A free strategy doc + data ask requires Don to make THREE commitments simultaneously (read, agree, expose data). Each is friction. Phase 0 collapses them into one transactional ask: pay $2.5K, get a useful report under NDA. Don can walk away after Phase 0 with nothing lost.
4. **Outcome-led pricing makes the math obvious.** Inquiry-to-first-class conversion is the headline metric. Going from 15% → 30% on 100 inquiries/mo is 15 incremental members at $1,500-$3,000 LTV = $22,500-$45,000 incremental LTV per month. Phase 1 ($9.5K) pays back inside 30 days at conservative assumptions.
5. **This engagement is the wedge for Sameer's AI Agency, not just a one-off.** The cross-system playbook (data discovery → wedge automation → growth retainer) generalizes to any NJ SMB with 2-5 locations and a vendor-dependency problem. Pricing, NDAs, and deliverable templates from this engagement become reusable assets.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Vision deck (12-month future-state pitch, light scope)

Highest wow, easiest to write. Doesn't solve the data-trust gap. Don reads it, says "love this," and you're back where you started.

### Approach B: Staged growth roadmap (Phase 0 / Phase 1 / Phase 2) — RECOMMENDED

Solves data reluctance structurally via Phase 0 (paid discovery under NDA). Each phase has defined exit. Maps to how Don already thinks about budget. More work to write but converts much higher.

### Approach C: Wedge-only proposal (single 30-day engagement, $8-12K)

Clean and simple. Doesn't paint the future. Hits the data-trust wall directly without a way around it.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B — Staged growth roadmap.**

Three phases:

- **Phase 0:** Discovery Sprint, 2 weeks, $2,500. NDA + data access + State-of-the-Membership report. Answers Don's stated question (members by location) in week 1.
- **Phase 1:** Acquisition Loop Build, 30 days, $9,500. Custom business phone, lead-to-first-class loop, deliverability cleanup, consent capture, conversion dashboard.
- **Phase 2:** Growth Engine Retainer, $4,000/mo ongoing. Reactivation, HyRox/PT integration, location segmentation, paid acquisition layer (the $10K campaign rideable), monthly reporting.

Pricing assumes Sameer's existing solo-consultant rate. Adjust if his actual day rate suggests different ranges.

## Open Questions

- **Don's actual vendor relationships:** Is the WL account manager genuinely just doing platform support, or is she informally consulting on growth strategy? If the latter, positioning needs sharpening.
- **Compliance reality on the $10K campaign:** The notes say "campaign approval takes a few weeks." That's likely 10DLC SMS registration. This creates a hard sequence — Phase 1 must complete BEFORE the $10K paid acquisition launches, or Phase 2 timing slips.
- **Membership accuracy ground truth:** "500 members" is a rough number. Phase 0 has to deliver the actual count. Sameer should not commit to growth metrics until Phase 0 establishes the denominator.
- **Sameer's pricing comfort:** $2,500 / $9,500 / $4,000 ranges are educated guesses based on solo NJ consultant rates. Sameer should sanity-check against his own day rate before sending.
- **Phase 0 deliverable timeline risk:** Getting WL API access through the account manager could take longer than 2 weeks. Fallback: one-time CSV export. Sameer should confirm this is acceptable to WL before proposing the timeline.

## Success Criteria

For Sameer's AI Agency wedge:

- **Don pays Phase 0 fee within 14 days of receiving the strategy doc.** This is the primary success signal. If he doesn't, the trust gap was bigger than diagnosed and the product needs reshaping.
- **Phase 0 delivers a usable members-by-location report in week 1.** This is the credibility opener that makes Phase 1 feel inevitable.
- **Phase 1 demonstrates measurable conversion improvement in 30 days.** Lead-to-first-class rate must move at least 50%, on real volume. Without this, Phase 2 doesn't get sold.
- **Engagement template generalizes.** Within 90 days of Phase 1 close, Sameer should be able to send a near-identical Phase 0 / Phase 1 / Phase 2 doc to a second SMB with minimal customization.

## Distribution Plan

The strategy doc itself is delivered as a PDF or Notion page Sameer hands to Don in person at the gym, OR sends as an email attachment with a 60-second voice note explaining the staged approach. In-person preferred — the doc is a prop for a conversation, not a standalone artifact.

Sameer's AI Agency repo (`ai_agency/`) becomes the home for: NDA templates, Phase 0 / 1 / 2 deliverable templates, pricing sheets, and the playbook for the next 10 SMB customers.

## Dependencies

- NDA template (one-pager, mutual, no-cost)
- Wellness Living API documentation (Sameer should pre-read before Phase 0 starts)
- Twilio account + 10DLC registration for Phase 1 SMS work
- Email deliverability tools (DMARC analyzer, e.g., dmarcian or postmark) for Phase 1
- A reusable Phase 0 report template (Sameer should build this in Notion or Markdown so future engagements get faster turnaround)

The real-world action that beats everything else: get the in-person 15-minute conversation. Don has shown he'll talk in person but ghost on email. Match the medium to his behavior.

## What I noticed about how you think

- **You hold contradictions honestly.** When I pushed on the demand-reality question, you didn't defend "Don asked me for a proposal" against the evidence. You went and got the actual quote ("let me know how much you charge") and named the surrounding messiness ("randomly he was like..."). That's mature signal-reading, not founder-cosplay confidence. Most people would have hidden the messiness.
- **You named the trust gap unprompted.** "He seems reluctant to share his membership data with me though" — you said that voluntarily, mid-flow, without me asking. That's the most important sentence of the session. Most consultants in your position would have ignored it because it complicates the pitch. You surfaced it because you want the actual answer, not the pretty one. That's the difference between a consultant who closes deals and one who eats glass.
- **Your default mode is solution-stacking.** When you said "first foundational data cleanup, then we can do really fancy things like hyper personalized segmentation marketing websites incorporating goal high-level things," you skipped from "the broken thing" to "the eventual platform" without nailing the wedge. This is a Garry-Tan-flagged failure mode for technical founders — solving the problem in your head before checking it against the buyer. The fix isn't to dream less; it's to gate the dream behind a specific Phase 1 outcome that Don pays for. The strategy doc does that for you.
- **You're closer to operating an agency than you think.** Your repo is literally `ai_agency`. Your three businesses are de facto consulting clients. This is the first ENGAGEMENT where you have to write the contract. Once you write it, the next nine SMBs in NJ are template-and-replace.
